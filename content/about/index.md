**Hi! Olá! 你好! Apa khabar?** I'm Ling Ling.

I'm a Doctoral Researcher and Intercultural Psychologist in social psychology focusing on how individuals and societies respond to intercultural relations, migration, particularly in relation to refugees. Essentially, I conduct research, write papers, give talks, and facilitate workshops. 

My PhD research focuses on **asylum-seekers and refugees' psychological and sociocultural adaptation** in resettled and transit countries using mixed methods, specifically interventions, meta-analyses, thematic analyses, and psychometrics. I am especially interested in how psychological processes shape responses to displacement in ways that have real-world social and policy implications. 

Before entering academia, I worked in engineering, travel technology, learning & development, and humanitarian organisations, across Europe, United States, and the Asia Pacific. I was exposed to cultural and societal challenges of discrimination and inequality in cross-cultural environments. As such, these experience continues to inform how I approach research questions and the kinds of problems I am drawn to.

I aim to conduct research that is both methodologically rigorous and socially responsible, particularly when working on topics involving vulnerable or displaced populations. In my research, I use a participatory approach to generate new knowledge that informs researchers and practitioners in their programmes and interventions. 

I am also a member of:
- [The Global Network of Psychologists for Human Rights (GNPHR)](https://humanrightspsychology.org)
- [The International Association for Cross-Cultural Psychology (IACCP)](https://www.iaccp.org)
- [The International Association for Intercultural Relations (IAIR)](https://intercultural-academy.net)
- [The International Council of Psychologists (ICP)](https://icpweb.org)

I am currently based at the [Centre for Psychological Research and Social Intervention (CIS)](https://www.cis.iscte-iul.pt/) at ISCTE-University Institute of Lisbon. I welcome opportunities for discussion and collaboration on research related to migration, intergroup relations, and social policy.
